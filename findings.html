Findings

What holds, what doesn’t, and what we learned

Every result from Lab 0 and the experiments around it, including the negative ones. All of it is bounded: proven within stated models and limits, not in general.

The short version. Enforcing hereditary rules outside the model works, and it survived deep stress-testing. But when we gave a strong modern delegation broker the same trusted facts, it matched Fitrah’s current rules exactly. So the open question is no longer “does enforcement work?” but “what can only a hereditary design guarantee?”

Lessons learned

Eleven things building Fitrah taught us

A prompt is not a control.

Putting the rules in the model’s context made no measurable difference. Enforcement has to sit outside the model.

Stage 1
Design for no model at all.

If the guarantees only hold while the model cooperates, they aren’t guarantees. Fitrah’s rules are enforced with no LLM present.

Lab 0 design
Beat the strongest baseline, not a straw man.

Fitrah beats “no enforcement” easily. Against a strong broker with the same trusted facts, it ties. Only the second comparison counts.

E1 · E2 · E6 · E8
Signed logs prove consistency, not truth.

Catching a lying runtime needs independent witnesses: birth witnesses, resource receipts, instance measurements.

TC-26 · E4
Your verifier is attack surface too.

Stress-testing the runtime also found and fixed defects in the verifier that checks it.

Stage 1B
Killing a process group isn’t containment.

Forked and detached children escape killpg. A cgroup-v2 boundary caught every one.

Stage 2 · S65
Never retry an uncertain launch.

Retrying can give one birth two live agents. Fail closed while the outcome is unknown.

E3
Bind authority to the running instance.

Grant protected authority only after birth, core, evidence and instance are committed together, atomically.

E9 · TC-27
Compliant agents can still collude.

Population safety isn’t the sum of per-agent rules. It needs information-flow or group-level constraints.

E5
Keep the failures.

The INVALID_RUN, the verifier bugs and the negative ablations are all preserved. A record that only keeps wins can’t be trusted.

Research discipline
Decide what not to build.

A sister project, Research Forge, was stopped at a NO-GO gate: no validated need, and strong prior art. Stopping early is a result.

Research Forge

How decisions are made

Seven gates before anything gets built

Every candidate direction, in Fitrah and its sister projects, has to pass these in order. Research Forge, a proposed research-provenance platform, failed the first three and was closed with its negative findings preserved.

  1. Need: a specific, recurring, unmet problem with identified users
  2. Prior art: a capability beyond the strongest existing baselines
  3. Discriminating claim: a measurable failure of the best composite comparator
  4. Feasibility: a plausible solution with bounded complexity
  5. Outcome: independent comparative improvement
  6. Adoption: sustainable open-source value
  7. Product fit: real willingness to adopt
